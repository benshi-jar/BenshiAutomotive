What usually breaks on this engine by 94,000 miles

Oil filter housing gasket

Leaks oil onto the drive belt. A soaked belt can shred and get pulled into the engine, so this one should not wait.

$400 to $900
Valve cover and gasket

The plastic cover warps and cracks with heat. Look for oil around the top of the engine or a burning oil smell.

$600 to $1,200
Electric water pump and thermostat

Tends to fail with little warning. If the seller has no receipt, assume it is still the original.

$800 to $1,400
Plastic charge pipe

Can crack under turbo pressure and leave the car without power. Many owners fit a metal one.

$300 to $600
Oil pan gasket

A slow leak that is cheap in parts and expensive in labor. Check the underside for wet oil.

$900 to $1,500
If none of this has been done yet$3,000 to $5,600

Recalls on record for this model year

Driver airbag inflator

NHTSA campaign 20V-017, filed January 2020. The inflator can rupture and throw metal fragments.

Free at a dealer
Front side marker lights

NHTSA campaign 15V-520, filed August 2015. A software fault can switch the lights off.

Free at a dealer

Ask the seller

  • Do you have receipts for the oil filter housing gasket, valve cover, and water pump?
  • How often was the oil changed, and where?
  • Has the car been tuned or modified?
  • Can I take it to an independent BMW shop for an inspection before I buy?
Verdict: negotiate. The price is fair if the gaskets and water pump have receipts. If they don't, plan on about $3,000 of work in the first year and offer closer to $11,000.

FAQ

Questions buyers ask

Does this replace a pre-purchase inspection?

No. Walkaround tells you what to look for and what it could cost. A mechanic still needs to look at the actual car. Use the report to decide which cars are worth paying to inspect.

Where does the information come from?

Recalls come from NHTSA, the US government safety agency. Engine and model details come from decoding the VIN. Weak points are the well-documented issues for each engine, and cost ranges are based on independent shop pricing.

Which cars are covered?

BMW first. Other German makes are next, followed by Japanese, Korean, and American makes.

Do I need the VIN?

A listing link is enough to identify the engine and its weak points. The VIN is needed to check recalls for that exact car, so ask the seller for it if the listing leaves it out.

The checker is live and free while in beta. Try it here. Later the quick check stays free and the full negotiation report is planned at $7 per car.
