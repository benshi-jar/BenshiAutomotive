Engine guides

The engine matters more than the badge

Two used BMWs can look identical and cost very different amounts to own. Pick the engine in the car you are looking at to see what usually breaks, when, and what it costs.

BMW N52low risk

3.0L inline-six, no turbo. The dependable choice. Known leaks and cooling parts, few expensive surprises.

BMW N54high risk

3.0L inline-six, twin turbo. Fast and loved by tuners, but the most repair-prone six here. Buy only with records.

BMW N55medium risk

3.0L inline-six, single turbo. Fairly reliable, with a predictable list of gasket and cooling repairs around 80,000 to 100,000 miles.

BMW N20high risk

2.0L four-cylinder turbo (N26 is the low-emissions version). Build date decides everything. Early engines can destroy themselves when the timing chain guides break.

BMW B48low risk

2.0L four-cylinder turbo (B46 is the low-emissions version). A big step up from the N20. Very few common problems so far.

BMW B58low risk

3.0L inline-six, single turbo. Widely regarded as one of the most reliable engines BMW has made. The safest turbo six to buy used.

BMW N62high risk

4.4L / 4.8L V8, no turbo. Cheap to buy, expensive to keep. Three known leaks that each cost four figures at a shop.

BMW N63very high risk

4.4L V8, twin turbo. The cheap V8 BMW is cheap for a reason. Avoid 2008-2013 engines unless the price leaves room for an engine.

BMW S65high risk

4.0L V8, no turbo (M3). A special engine with one big question: have the rod bearings been replaced, and when.

BMW S55medium risk

3.0L inline-six, twin turbo (M2 Competition, M3, M4). Solid for an M engine. The famous crank hub issue is rare on stock cars.
